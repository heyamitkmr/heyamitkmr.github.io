The Fermi Estimator’s Core Playbook 6 RULES
1. Error Cancellation (The Central Engine): If you break an intractable problem into 5 sub-variables and estimate each within a factor of 3, your errors log-normally balance out. Expected total drift scales with √N, not N.
2. Supply vs. Demand Decomposition: Decide whether to count consumers (demand) or capacity/providers (supply). Pick whichever side has fewer moving parts.
Restaurant Revenue: Demand = Diners × Average Bill. Supply = Seats × Turns × Average Bill.
3. Geometric Mean Bounding: When blind on a variable, establish a lower bound that feels definitely too low ($L$) and an upper bound that is definitely too high ($H$).
Estimate = √(L × H). (e.g. between 100 and 10,000 → $\sqrt{10^2 \times 10^4} = 1,000$).
4. Little’s Law for Flows: For queueing, pipes, transit, or in-flight objects:
L = λ × W (Units in System = Arrival Rate × Average Residence Time).
5. Strict One Significant Figure: Round relentlessly mid-flight ($365 \to 400$; $\pi \to 3$; $68\% \to 70\%$). Extra precision is cognitive waste and gives false confidence.

Worked Walkthrough: Commercial Planes in the Air CASE STUDY

Question: How many commercial passenger airliners are airborne globally right now?

Structural Equation (Little's Law):
Planes Airborne (L) = Total Daily Flights (λ) / 24 hrs × Average Flight Duration (W)

Step 1: Estimate Global Flight Volume

  • US has ~30 major hub airports (ATL, ORD, LAX, JFK...).
  • US represents ~25% of commercial aviation → Worldwide ~120 mega-hubs + ~380 active regional airports ≈ 500 commercial airports.
  • Average busy runway does 1 movement every 2 mins ≈ 30 ops/hr. Over a 24-hr cycle (busy peak + quiet night), average 20 departures/hour/airport.
  • Departures per day: 500 airports × 20 departures/hr × 24 hr ≈ ~100,000 to 200,000 flights/day. Let's calibrate to 100,000 flights/day.

Step 2: Departure Rate (λ)

λ = 100,000 flights / 24 hours ≈ 4,000 flights/hour

Step 3: Average Duration (W)

Short hops (1 hr), transcontinental (3-5 hr), long-haul international (8-12 hr). Domestic flights dominate passenger volume. Weighted average: 2.5 hours.

Result L = 4,000 × 2.5 = 10,000 planes in the air

Flightradar24 tracking confirms typical global airborne commercial peak is 10,000 to 14,000 planes. Accuracy: Within 25%.
