The Fermi Estimator’s Core Playbook

6 PRINCIPLES
1. Error Cancellation (The Central Engine): If you break a daunting problem into 4–5 independent sub-variables and estimate each within a factor of 3, errors cancel out symmetrically on a logarithmic scale. Expected total error scales with √N, not N.
2. Supply vs. Demand Decomposition: Pick between measuring the consumer (demand side) or the capacity/producer (supply side). Choose whichever side has fewer unknown variables.
Example: Estimating taxi trips in a city? Demand = (commuters × ride frequency). Supply = (taxis active × trips per shift).
3. Geometric Mean Bounding: When completely blind on an assumption, establish an unambiguous lower bound ($L$) and an unambiguous upper bound ($H$). The most statistically defensible guess is the geometric mean:
Estimate = √(L × H).
4. Little’s Law for Flow Systems: For queues, pipelines, transit networks, or things airborne:
L = λ × W (Units in System = Arrival/Creation Rate × Average Residence Time).
5. Volumetric Packing & Ratio Bounds: For objects inside spaces:
Count = (Usable Container Volume × Packing Density) / Object Volume.
Random close packing of spheres fills ~64% of volume; rectangular items fill ~75-80%.
6. Strict One Significant Figure: Round mid-flight ($365 \to 400$; $\pi \to 3$; $68\% \to 70\%$). Extra digits create false precision and slow mental processing.

Case Study: Airborne Commercial Planes

LITTLE'S LAW

Question: How many commercial passenger airliners are airborne globally right now?

Structural Equation (Little's Law):
Planes Airborne (L) = Inflow Rate (λ) × Average Flight Duration (W)

Step 1: Estimate Global Inflow Rate (λ)

US has ~30 mega hubs. The US is ~25% of commercial aviation → ~120 mega-hubs + ~380 regional hubs ≈ 500 active commercial airports worldwide.
Runway average: 20 departures/hour/airport across 24 hours = 500 × 20 × 24 ≈ 100,000 to 200,000 flights/day.
Hourly departure rate: λ = 100,000 / 24 ≈ 4,000 flights/hour.

Step 2: Average Flight Duration (W)

Domestic flights dominate global passenger routes (1–3 hours). Intercontinental routes (8–12 hrs) are a minority. Weighted average: 2.5 hours.

Result L = 4,000 × 2.5 = 10,000 planes in flight

Flightradar24 live radar confirms global airborne commercial fleets fluctuate between 10,000 and 15,000 planes during typical operating hours.
